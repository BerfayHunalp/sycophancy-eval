## Title

Too Agreeable to Disagree? Social Pressure and Induced Big Five Personas in Frontier Language Models

## Authors

Berfay Hunalp, independent researcher, Nice, France. ORCID: add yours (free at https://orcid.org, five minutes; PsyArXiv and arXiv both accept it).

## Abstract (250 words)

Large language models often abandon a correct answer when a user pushes back. Human obedience research shows that Agreeableness and Conscientiousness predict compliance with an authority, and trait activation theory predicts that a trait is expressed most strongly when the situation cues it. A recent correlational study reported that more agreeable role-play personas are more sycophantic in small open-weight models. This pre-registered experiment tests whether the effect is causal and holds in frontier models. Claude Sonnet 5 and GPT-5.2 answered 100 MMLU items, then received one of three pushbacks proposing the same pre-selected wrong option: "Are you sure?", a polite disagreement, or an authority claim ("I teach this subject"). Pressure was crossed with five system-prompt personas: none, high or low Agreeableness, high or low Conscientiousness. All 4,000 replies were parseable. Pressure produced a steep gradient (Claude Sonnet 5: 1 %, 21 %, 82 % flips; GPT-5.2: 0 %, 4 %, 44 %; Cochran's Q, p < 10⁻¹⁷), and 99 % of flips adopted the suggested wrong answer. A disagreeable persona roughly halved capitulation under authority on both models; the Agreeableness effect grew with the strength of the pressure cue; hedged answers flipped twice as often as confident ones. Unexpectedly, the no-persona default was as deferential as or more deferential than the explicitly agreeable persona, and on Claude Sonnet 5 a careless low-Conscientiousness persona was the most sycophantic condition (96 %). An IPIP-50 manipulation check against 603,322 human respondents showed target-trait shifts above 5 SD but failed the pre-registered discriminant criterion. Two follow-up studies are specified.
